Cybersecurity incidents disclosed to the SEC by US-listed companies

A record of what was disclosed, and by whom. Facts and links only — no interpretation, and no retelling of press coverage.

What is in this section, and what is not
  • The automatic layer is SEC Form 8-K Item 1.05 only. That item is filed when the company itself has determined a cybersecurity incident is material. Taking only 1.05 is deliberate: the company decides what counts, not this site.
  • Japan, Australia and Europe contribute nothing automatically. This is not because fewer incidents happen there. No such jurisdiction publishes a structured, per-incident feed. Australia's OAIC, for instance, publishes statistics but no per-incident record — a count of zero here would mean “cannot be retrieved”, never “did not happen”. For that reason this section shows no totals and no per-region counts.
  • Item 1.05 binds listed companies only. Private companies, government bodies, non-profits and healthcare providers file no 8-K. They reach this page only through the state registries below, and only if they filed in one of those two states.
  • The state registries are two states, not a country. California and Washington publish every breach notification filed with them, whoever filed it. But a filing appears only when residents of that state were affected, the two lists overlap where an organisation notified both, and the other forty-eight states are absent. The two are therefore never added together, and neither is added to the SEC layer.
  • Healthcare has no federal source here. The HHS breach portal is not machine-readable without browser automation, and collecting it was deferred — not attempted and abandoned. Healthcare organisations appear only when they filed in California or Washington.
  • The curated layer is not comprehensive. It holds incidents an editor chose to record, and nothing follows from an incident being absent from it.

Breach notifications organisations were required by state law to file. Collected daily. One row is one filing to one state, so an organisation that notified both states appears twice — those rows are not merged, because deciding they describe the same event would be a judgement this section does not make. Nothing here is edited after it is recorded.
California publishes the organisation, the breach dates and the date it was reported — no count of people affected. Washington publishes those, plus the number of Washington residents affected, the categories of data involved in the state's own wording, and a link to the notification document the organisation itself filed. A blank cell means the state does not publish that field, never that the value is zero.
Both lists publish after a delay: measured on 2026-08-10, California's newest entry was 3 days old and Washington's 13. A recent incident being absent means it has not been published yet, not that it was not filed.
